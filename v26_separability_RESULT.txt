POST-HOC OBSERVATIONS (not pre-registered; 9 splits; descriptive)
 - Across the 9 splits, the per-split Delta P1 at gain 2 correlates weakly with the stage-2 H differences: Spearman 0.42 with Rk2 - Rk, 0.07 with Rk2 - Bg4, -0.07 with Rk4 - Bg4. The two splits with the largest stage-2 losses (C3 fold 1 and fold 3) do not share a separability change: C3 f1 -0.009, C3 f3 -0.029. This gives no support for separability as the reason for the stage-2 H differences.
 - The direction is the same in all 27 models and the dose trend is monotone (-0.004, -0.016, -0.052 at gain 1, 2, 4), but the pre-registered 23/27 criterion with the noise margin is not reached at gain 2. The pre-registered verdict is not relabelled.

READING AND LIMITS
 - The standard-augmentation view of the VMD-perturbed window separates the unknown fault classes slightly less well than the standard view of the original window (about 0.016 AUROC at gain 2) in a fixed warm-up feature space. The exposure indicator shows that a larger share of the VMD budget falls on modes that are informative for the unknown classes than for the known classes. The two observations are consistent with H2, but they do not show that exposure caused the loss of separability, and the screening rule for the loss is not met. The exposure indicator uses the allocated budget, not the damage done to a mode; the known and unknown groups differ in number of classes, windows and installations.
 - Warm-up encoder (300 steps); the trained Chapter-3 encoder may behave differently. Separability in a fixed feature space does not show an effect on H. 200,000 pairs share windows and recordings; 27 models are 9 splits x 3 seeds, not independent datasets.
 - If a protection of exposed modes were ever designed, its training-time rule may not use true unknown labels (they are used here only for diagnosis). That would need a new pre-registration; none is planned.
