READING AND LIMITS (descriptive; added after the result)
 1. The pre-registered "intensity matched" criterion is satisfied by signal change and log-spectral distance, but it does not cover the encoder's response: the coordinated view moves the feature about 5 times less. Any end-to-end comparison of independent versus coordinated perturbation at "matched intensity" would therefore still be confounded in feature space, and the coordinated arm would effectively be a much weaker perturbation to the encoder. The criterion would need a feature-level (or encoder-sensitivity) matching rule in a new pre-registration.
 2. Dnat is not a clean between-recording scale: 75 % of its value is the within-recording fluctuation of the relation (0.40), and each recording has few labelled windows. Dv(VI)/within-recording = 1.17. The independent perturbation moves the relation about as much as two windows of the same recording differ from each other; the 0.25 criterion is met by a wide margin (0.86), but it is relative to a scale dominated by window-level variation.
 3. Dv(VC) being much smaller than Dv(VI) follows largely from the construction (shared random numbers); the remaining 0.153 comes from unequal channel budgets and the shrink rule. This is an integrity check, as pre-registered, not evidence of usefulness.
 4. The coordinated and independent views have the same per-channel marginal law and signal-level intensity by design. Whether the encoder's lower response to the coordinated view is a benefit (less damage) or a loss (less augmentation) is not tested here.
 5. Band gain is a magnitude-only perturbation; it is not VMD. The VMD novelty claim of Chapter 3 does not transfer. Warm-up encoders; fixed feature spaces say nothing about H. 27 models are 9 splits x 3 seeds, not independent datasets.
 6. No end-to-end run, no H, no repositioning decision follows from this result by itself.
